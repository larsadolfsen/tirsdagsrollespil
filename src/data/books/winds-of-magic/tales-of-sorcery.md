# Tales of Sorcery

A scene-setting chapter on the history and public perception of magic in the Empire, and on how the Colleges of Magic police arcane corruption. Mostly flavour; the reference content below is the useful part for play.

## A History of Magic

Norscan legend holds that Tzeentch shattered into crystal shards when cast down by the other Chaos Gods; every spell a mortal wizard learns is said to feed a shard back into his Impossible Fortress. Southern scholars dismiss this as heresy, but it colours why Chaos and magic are linked in the popular imagination.

Slann Mage-Priest lore traces true magic to the Old Ones, who arrived before recorded history, opened Warp gates at the poles, and built the Geomantic Web (leylines) to tame the Winds. Around –5600 IC the Warp gates collapsed, flooding the world with raw magic, spawning Chaos, Morrslieb, and warpstone, and dooming the first generation of Slann and Elves to centuries of containment work.

The Elven mage Caledor Dragontamer and the Phoenix King Aenarion later fought the Chaos hordes this cataclysm unleashed. When the war could not be won by arms alone, Caledor sacrificed himself to complete the Great Ritual, creating the Great Vortex on Ulthuan that continuously drains excess magic from the world — the same Vortex that later underpins the Colleges' waystone network.

Magic was outlawed in the Empire from Sigmar's founding decree onward, enforced by witch hunters (notably the Order of the Silver Hammer) with centuries of persecution, hidden hedge wizards, and periodic amnesties or corrupt tolerance under weak Emperors. This only ended when Magnus the Pious, facing the Great Incursion of Chaos in 2302 IC, accepted the Elf archmage Teclis's offer to train Human wizards as a weapon against Chaos.

## Founding of the Colleges of Magic

Teclis and two fellow Loremasters screened Magnus's amnestied wizards, executing the corrupted and training the rest in a safer, disciplined method. The survivors became the first Magisters. Teclis founded eight Colleges of Magic (one per Wind) in Altdorf's Wizards' Quarter ("Hexxerbezrik"), with a Patriarch heading each College and a Supreme Patriarch (first Volans) speaking for all of them to the Emperor. The Colleges' self-policing kept the witch hunters mostly at bay.

Notable incidents referenced in College lore: **Paranoth's Wheel**, the (contested) colour-coded diagram of the eight Lores devised by a Jade Supreme Patriarch; the **Night of the Black Waltz**, an unexplained mass-death dancing plague in Altdorf blamed on drunken rioting; and the **Night of a Thousand Arcane Duels** (2415 IC), a magical civil war during the octennial Supreme Patriarch duel that got the Colleges closed for 15 years.

## Magic Today and College Recruitment

All Humans have some magical attuning and are technically eligible to join a College; priority goes to those already showing Second Sight or spontaneous cantrips (typically manifesting age 15–25). Journeymen scout the Empire for gifted recruits, who are sent to Altdorf or a preparatory school. The Articles of Imperial Magic require Colleges to accept any magically gifted applicant not already corrupted beyond redemption — a College Patriarch can order a master to take on an at-risk apprentice.

College wizards understand waystones and leylines (which refract, dampen, and redirect the Winds) far better than laypeople, but common folk and even priests remain largely ignorant and superstitious about magic, and rumours of conspiracy persist. Divine miracles and arcane magic overlap in ways that are debated rather than settled.

## Arcane Corruption

The Articles of Imperial Magic make College wizards responsible for identifying and resolving arcane corruption (an excess of Dhar), whether Chaotic or Necromantic in origin, and require them to drop other commitments to deal with it. Colleges may lead a corruption purge or advise witch hunters, priests, or the military, and often collaborate with High Elf advisors on leyline corruption. Destroying the root source of the corruption is normally the top priority; fire is often the only practical cure once corruption has taken hold.

- **Perceiving corruption**: a wizard may spot subtle early signs of corruption with a **Hard (-20) Perception Test**, before it manifests visibly. Individuals with the Second Sight Talent perceive magical phenomena according to their own background and training.
- Chaotic corruption tends to show through erratic strands of colour magic bleeding into the Dhar; Necromantic corruption is easier to identify, since it's simply concentrated Dhar.

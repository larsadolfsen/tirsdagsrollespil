# Magical Artefacts

Common trappings used by spellcasters in their day-to-day work: robes, staffs, scrolls, potions, grimoires, and power stones, followed by a scattering of notable one-off curiosities and cursed items. A wizard, witch, or hedge witch wearing robes for a Lore they don't practice suffers -1 SL to all Casting and Channelling.

## Wizard's Robes

Robes give a bonus to Channelling Tests based on grade, and mark a wizard's rank. College officials issue a Practical Robe to a Wizard, a Standard Robe to a Master Wizard, and an Elaborate Robe to a Wizard Lord. Chaos sorcerers, necromancers, and daemonologists must commission their own from an unscrupulous tailor; witches and hedge witches get no bonus from robes at all.

## Enchanted Staffs

Most licensed magisters receive an Enchanted Staff from their tutor on completing their apprenticeship (lost/broken ones cost 15 GC to replace via Commission). While borne, it reduces the CN of spells from its associated Lore (and any Arcane spells) by 1, to a minimum of 0, and acts as an extension of the wielder's body for touch-range spells. It can still be swung as a Quarterstaff.

Elven mage-smiths can attune a single staff to multiple Winds without drawing in Dhar; Human wizards cannot, so a multi-Lore Human wizard must either carry a single-Lore staff or one attuned to Dhar (which forces a daily Corruption Test).

## Scrolls

A scroll lets even a non-wizard cast a bound spell once, by reading its command words aloud and passing a Challenging (+0) Language (Magick) Test (or a Hard (-20) Language Test in the scroll's written language, if the reader isn't a wizard). Success casts the spell and destroys the scroll; failure wastes nothing and can be retried next round. Scrolls always count as cast with an ingredient for Miscast purposes — but scrolls of Witchcraft still count as Minor Exposure to Corruption, and scrolls of Daemonology, Necromancy, or Chaos Magic count as Moderate Exposure.

To write a scroll, a spellcaster with Read/Write and a writing kit must know the spell, pass an Extended Challenging (+0) Art (Writing) Test to 20 SL, then cast the spell without miscasting (failure ruins the scroll). Binding a ritual instead requires 40 SL and doubles the ritual's CN for the casting.

A random found scroll can be generated by rolling twice: once for Lore, once for a maximum CN, picking the GM's choice of matching spell (or the lowest-CN spell in the Lore if none fit).

## Magic Potions

Magic potions are brewed by channelling a Wind into natural ingredients; alchemical, herbal, and apothecary methods can approximate the same effects with different costs, reliability, and Skills (Trade (Alchemist)/(Herbalist)/(Apothecary) respectively). Only the magical method requires Channelling and produces the widest, most volatile range of effects.

- **Requirements**: a Laboratory (100 GC; Poor Quality 50 GC at -1 SL to Brewing; Fine Quality 200 GC at +1 SL), the ingredients, and a recipe (rare, and priced by negotiation).
- **Brewing**: assemble ingredients (bought, or foraged with a Lore (Herbs) Test modified by Ingredient Difficulty), then spend the potion's Creation Time (at least 3 hours/day) before making a Brewing Test modified by approach and the potion's Volatility (Minor/Moderate/Major/Extreme) and Creation Difficulty. Second Sight gives +1 SL to Brewing.
- Success yields 1d10 doses (double on a Critical). Failure wastes the attempt; a Fumble triggers a Brewing Disaster (ranging from a spoiled batch, through minor injury, up to the laboratory catching fire or being destroyed).
- **Spoilage**: every potion has a chance to spoil based on the brewer's SL and the potion's age in seasons; herbalist potions spoil fastest, alchemical potions slowest. A spoiled potion either does nothing or triggers a Spoilage Effect scaled to the potion's Volatility — dozens of minor-to-extreme afflictions exist (rashes, hallucinations, poison, temporary sensory loss, and at the extreme end, Deadly poison or Apparent Death). GMs should keep the exact outcome hidden from players until it manifests.
- The **Concoct Talent** lets a character use Trade (Apothecary) or Trade (Alchemist) to brew once per session without a workshop or laboratory.

## Grimoires

A grimoire holds roughly four spells (or one ritual), written in Lingua Praestantia, and lets a wizard cast directly from the book without having memorised the spell — at double CN, requiring both hands free. Wizards may only cast from a grimoire for Lores they know; college rules forbid mixing multiple Lores in one grimoire (Arcane and Petty spells are always permitted alongside a single Lore).

Transcribing a known spell into a grimoire takes 1 hour per CN plus 1 hour (reducible with a successful Art (Writing) Test); a non-wizard Read/Write holder can copy one with a slower Extended Test.

Fumbling a casting from a grimoire triggers an additional roll on the **Grimoire Miscast Table**: results range from no effect (most common), through painful backlash (with a chance the spell is instantly memorised), to the spell burning out of the book, or — rarely — the grimoire's outright destruction.

Some grimoires are famous artefacts in their own right: *The Writings of Sedelmann* secretly channels Life spells while inflicting Minor Exposure to Corruption on every use; *The Book of WA* is a scattered forty-page collection of dogma-challenging essays and spells, its lost volumes still sought by scholars; *The Hidden Books of Chamon* are magic items in their own right, written in ink that only reveals itself near strong Chamon; *Krampi's Tome of Power* holds thirteen mighty spells, one or more of which is booby-trapped to injure a careless caster.

### Random Grimoires

A found grimoire can be randomly generated by rolling for physical Type (book, scroll, box of scrolls), total Spells and Max CN, associated Lore, and two Characteristics chosen from a table of quirks — e.g. poorly-written (raises CN), lockable or trapped, decorated and valuable, coded (must be deciphered), or carrying a hidden Corrupting Influence sewn into the binding.

## Power Stones

Gems that condense a single Wind of Magic, pioneered by Teclis's Orbs of Sorcery and later imitated more crudely by the Colleges (the Golden Order's "Goldstones," refined from Habermas's original Philosopher's Stone). Each College favours a signature stone, e.g. Fire Rubies (Aqshy/Bright) or Ghost Amber (Ghur/Amber).

- **Casting Spells**: holding a Power Stone gives +3 SL to a Casting Test for a spell of its matching Wind; the stone is consumed after one use, and can be combined with an Ingredient if held in the other hand.
- **Binding Spirits**: a Minor Elemental can be ritually bound into a Power Stone, retaining its Suffuse with (Wind) Talent, which then extends to the stone.
- **Magical Devices**: a Power Stone with a bound Elemental can store one Colour Magic spell, imprinted by casting it into the stone; releasing it later needs only a spoken command word (no Casting Test), the spell resolving at +0 SL.
- **Releasing Bound Spirits**: shattering the stone frees the elemental, which will generally obey its original binder if present.
- A stone's inner glow (visible to Second Sight) fades as its stored energy expires, typically within a year or two.

## Notable Curiosities

A handful of items drawn from the (dubious) catalogue of the vanished collector Aygar Mistletaine — treat these as adventure hooks or GM-awarded oddities rather than purchasable trappings.

### The Hammer of Glorri

A talking magical hammer, potent against Goblins and Snotlings, that curses loudly and profanely whenever such creatures are near — making it a poor choice for any job requiring stealth.

### Eyeglasses of Unknown (but Almost Certainly Dwarf) Manufacture

Finely ground lenses in gold frames, etched with tiny Dwarf runes, granting the wearer remarkably sharp eyesight.

### The Bone of Want

A small rib bone that regenerates any meat eaten from it overnight — only a few mouthfuls at a time, but never-ending.

### An Ironic Locket

A locket sealing a curl of hair that repels vampires, said to require a taste of the wearer's blood to activate — sold by a con artist with delusions of real sorcery.

### Myselia's Gnarly Staff

The staff (and embedded false teeth) of a hedge witch who turned to Necromancy after plague devastated her village, then vanished; possibly cursed.

### The Avid Knife

A Dark Elf blade that drains a victim's lifeforce into its wielder, then heals that same victim afterward — ensuring they survive to be fed on again and again.

### The Half-Starved

A hungry Dark Elf blade sustained by blood and souls; it empowers its wielder in battle but, if never sated, eventually drains them dry as well.

## Cursed Items

Every cursed item carries the Cursed and Magical Qualities, and is defined by a **Boon** (its benefit), a **Bane** (the curse), and a **Trigger** (what sets the Bane off). Curses usually can't be safely removed by simply discarding the item — many owners keep using a cursed item anyway, in a bargain that accepts the Bane for the sake of the Boon.

### Axe of Unrelenting Fury

**Boon**: grants the Frenzy Talent. **Trigger**: failing a Willpower Test (increasingly likely each use in a fight) after causing Wounds. **Bane**: the resulting Frenzy doesn't end when enemies run out — the wielder must attack the nearest living creature, friend or foe, until disarmed, Stunned, or knocked unconscious.

### Boots of Sudden Remorse

**Boon**: silent, trackless footsteps. **Trigger**: any Stealth Test. **Bane**: the boots emit a loud squeak, an automatic Astounding Failure, even if merely carried on the wearer's person.

### Bow of Bloody Empathy

**Boon**: +20 Ranged (Bow). **Trigger**: a successful hit at 2 SL or less. **Bane**: the bow injures its archer (snapping string, awkward recoil), inflicting 1 Bleeding Condition.

### Brooch of Unwanted Attraction

**Boon**: none. **Trigger**: an opponent's missed blow. **Bane**: an inverted Ward — on a roll of 7+ (d10), the "missed" blow actually strikes home with 1+ SL.

### Dagger of Stolen Luck

**Boon**: +20 Melee (Basic). **Trigger**: a successful strike at 2 SL or less. **Bane**: steals 1 Fortune Point from the wielder.

### Fellowship Sunderer

**Boon**: +30 Melee (Basic) (hammer). **Trigger**: a successful attack at 3 SL or less. **Bane**: rings out a deafening clang, inflicting 1 Wound (ignoring Toughness/Armour) on everyone within 20 feet, with a chance of Deafened or Bleeding on repeat occurrences.

### Flail of Unsolicited Attention

**Boon**: +10 Melee (Flail). **Trigger**: any attack made with it. **Bane**: enemies preferentially gang up on the wielder (granting them Advantage for outnumbering), and the wielder loses all levels of Combat Master while using it.

### Knuckles of Ignominy

**Boon**: Impale Quality. **Trigger**: causing a Critical Hit. **Bane**: the wielder automatically contracts a random, fast-resolving but socially humiliating disease.

### Mail of Stolen Valour

**Boon**: 3 Armour Points (Body/Arms). **Trigger**: failing a Cool Test after the armour absorbs a hit (difficulty worsens each subsequent hit). **Bane**: the wearer must flee combat (0 AP while fleeing) until they pass a Willpower Test.

### Pistol of Involuntary Solitude

**Boon**: +20 Ranged (Pistol). **Trigger**: a successful hit at 2 SL or less. **Bane**: emits foul-smelling blackpowder smoke that clings for 24 hours, imposing -30 Fellowship.

### Sword of Holding

**Boon**: +20 Melee (Basic), Hack Quality. **Trigger**: a successful strike at 2 SL or less. **Bane**: the blade lodges in the victim's armour or flesh, requiring a Hard (-20) Strength Test and an action to remove — leaving the victim dangerously exposed meanwhile.

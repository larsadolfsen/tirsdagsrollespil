# Arcane Activities

New Careers, Skills, and Alchemy rules connected to the practice of magic in the Empire — the non-College-wizard trades that brush up against the Winds.

## New Careers

Four new supporting Careers, plus context for the eight College Wizard Careers detailed elsewhere in this book. A Character entering the Wizard Career can choose the general Core Rulebook version or one of this book's College-specific Wizard Careers; either way they must affiliate with a College and take on its arcane marks and Lore.

### Beadle

*Dwarf, Halfling, Human — a college's practical guard, groundskeeper, and enforcer, freeing academics to focus on research.*

Career path: **Laboratory Assistant** (Silver 1) → **Beadle** (Silver 2) → **Groundskeeper** (Silver 4) → **Terror of the Faculty** (Silver 5). Key skills build from Dodge/Stealth/Melee toward Intimidate, Leadership, and Lore; talents run from Read/Write and Savvy up to Frightening and Unshakeable. Trappings scale from a faculty uniform to a breastplate and a crew of subordinate beadles.

Beadles run day-to-day college logistics — supplies, maintenance, discipline — and their workload varies wildly between sleepy colleges and the busy University of Altdorf faculties.

### Mundane Alchemist

*Dwarf, Halfling, Human — a chemist and materials expert on the edge of true magic.*

Career path: **Tinkerer** (Brass 3) → **Alchemist** (Silver 2) → **Master Alchemist** (Silver 3) → **Transmutator** (Gold 1). Skills progress from Trade (Alchemist/Poisoner) and Lore (Chemistry/Geology/Science) to Channelling (Chamon) and Ride; talents run from Concoct and Craftsman up to Petty Magic, Second Sight, and Arcane Magic (Lore of Metal). A Transmutator has a fine laboratory and a letter of commission.

Some alchemists develop a sense for Chamon (the Gold Wind) without recognising it as magic; a few join the Golden Order outright. Dwarf and Halfling Alchemists cannot become spellcasters and may not take Petty Magic or Arcane Magic (Lore of Metal).

**Alchemists as spellcasters**: an Alchemist who takes Petty Magic or Arcane Magic (Lore of Metal) is limited, until they enter a proper Wizard Career, to: Petty Magic — Bearings, Open Lock, Shock, Warning; Arcane Spells — Mundane Aura, Ward; Lore of Metal — Enchant Weapon, Fool's Gold, Forge of Chamon, Mutable Metal.

### Magister Vigilant

*Human — a College's secret enforcer, hunting down rogue and oath-broken wizards.*

Career path: **Vigilant's Apprentice** (Brass 4) → **Magister Vigilant** (Silver 4) → **Magister Inquisitor** (Gold 1) → **Lord Vigilant** (Gold 2). Skills move from Channelling, Language (Magick), and Melee toward Intimidate, Stealth, and Lore; talents run from Magical Sense and Second Sight up to Arcane Magic, Shadow, and Iron Will.

Each College quietly sponsors a small cadre of Magisters Vigilant (the Grey College reputedly the most); they combine spy, investigator, and executioner roles, answer only to their College head, and have an uneasy rivalry with witch hunters, who want public displays where Magisters Vigilant prefer discretion.

### Scryer

*Human — someone with the rare, difficult-to-control ability to read the past from people, places, or objects.*

Career path: **Haunted** (Brass 1) → **Scryer** (Brass 3) → **Psychometrician** (Silver 2) → **Reader of the Past** (Gold 1). Skills move from Outdoor Survival/Perception/Psychometry toward Bribery, Evaluate, and Leadership; talents run from Luck, Orientation, and Second Sight up to Sixth Sense, Magical Sense, and Commanding Presence.

Scryers are courted by nobles for information but risk being mistaken for witches; charlatans posing as scryers are common and often more popular, since they tell clients what they want to hear.

## New Arcane Skills

Two new Advanced Skills — **Augury** and **Psychometry** — cover fortune-telling and psychic-impression abilities that resemble spellcasting to onlookers but don't actually work the Winds directly, though both draw on a latent connection to magic. A Character may not have both Skills, and (unless starting as a Scryer) only Humans and Elves may learn Psychometry; only certain Careers (Mystic, Hedge Witch, Warrior Priest, Witch, Nun, Priest, Wizard, Scryer) may spend XP on it otherwise, and taking it at character creation costs a random Species Talent.

### Augury (Int)

Represents any of several fortune-telling traditions (Morrite prophetic dreams, Celestial star-reading, card/dice mystics, consulting the Thaumodivinator device in Altdorf, or bargaining with prophecy-minded Daemons). Usable once per day; Tests made on another's behalf are Difficult (-10).

**Augury Table**

| SL | Result | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| +6 or more | Astounding Success | Choose two symbols from the Symbol Table. |
| +4 to +5 | Impressive Success | Roll twice on the Symbol Table. |
| +2 to +3 | Success | Choose a symbol from the Symbol Table. |
| +0 to +1 | Marginal Success | Roll once on the Symbol Table. |
| –0 to –1 | Marginal Failure | No information received. |
| –2 to –3 | Failure | Roll once on the Symbol Table; the symbol is reversed. |
| –4 to –5 | Impressive Failure | Roll twice; both symbols reversed. |
| –6 or less | Astounding Failure | Roll three times; all symbols reversed. |

Each symbol drawn grants +1 SL (or –1 SL, if reversed) to the first Test the Character makes involving the Skill(s) tied to that symbol (e.g. Morrslieb → Perception, reversed → Corruption resistance; Sigmar → Leadership, reversed → Channelling; The Daemon → Channelling/Charm/Language (Magick), reversed → Leadership). A repeated symbol wastes the extra roll.

Celestial Wizards can spend spare Overcasting SL on prophetic spells (Fate's Fickle Fingers, Starcrossed, the three Portents of Amul) to roll on the Symbol Table instead of the spell's normal bonus effect; a miscast while casting such a spell can also saddle the target with a reversed symbol.

**The Thaumodivinator**: a coin-operated fortune-telling device in Altdorf (Golden Order housing, Celestial Order enchantment). A petitioner may make a Difficult (-10) Intelligence Test (Average (+20) with Read/Write) in place of an Augury Test, using the same tables.

**Intoxicants**: Dreamwine (a rare, banned, Chaos-tainted syrup) and chewed Weirdroot both let non-Augurs attempt a vision via an Intelligence Test (Challenging (+0) to fail into visions for Dreamwine after a Consume Alcohol Test, Hard (-20) for Weirdroot) in place of an Augury Test, generally with side effects (Corruption exposure, addiction-like "the call" for Dreamwine, or stat penalties for Weirdroot).

### Psychometry (Int)

The innate scryer's ability to receive impressions from a place, a touched object, or a touched person's unspoken thoughts. Every Psychometry Test, regardless of result, also forces an Average (+20) Endurance Test or a Fatigued Condition — the Skill is physically taxing.

**Psychometry Result Table**

| SL | Result | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| +6 or more | Astounding Success | At least three pieces of information, one highly pertinent. |
| +4 to +5 | Impressive Success | Two pieces of information. |
| +2 to +3 | Success | One piece of information. |
| +0 to +1 | Marginal Success | A vague, metaphorical impression. |
| –0 to –1 | Marginal Failure | No information received. |
| –2 to –3 | Failure | A vaguely misleading piece of information. |
| –4 to –5 | Impressive Failure | Disturbing, misleading impressions. |
| –6 or less | Astounding Failure | Overwhelmed by nightmarish impressions; gain 1 Corruption point. |

## Alchemy

Trade (Alchemy) requires a laboratory, or the Concoct Talent in its absence. A Fumbled Trade (Alchemy) Test rolls 1d10 + degrees of failure on the Brewing Disasters Table (Core Rulebook, p.161); High Alchemy Fumbles instead roll 1d10+3.

**Mundane (Low) Alchemy** — open to anyone with Trade (Alchemy) — isolates elements and produces simple compounds (soap, dye, ink, blackpowder, and similar). Producing a batch takes a preparation period plus a Challenging (+0) Trade (Alchemy) Test; extra SL increases yield by up to 5%/SL, capped at +20%.

**Alchemical Products** (selected): a Keg of Blackpowder (Challenging, 3 days); Improved Blackpowder (Difficult, 5 days — improves reload time and misfire chance, but its own misfires deal more damage); Bars of Soap and Vials of Dye (Difficult); a Magnet or Compass (Hard, 1–3 days); a Prism, Telescope, or Reading Lens (Very Hard, 1–2 weeks); Vials of Caustic/Corrosive Liquid (Difficult, thrown like an Incendiary).

**Al-kahest**, the universal solvent, needs an Extended Hard (-20) Trade (Alchemy) Test (20 SL) after first preparing a corrosive substance; a single vial can melt through six feet of stone or ten inches of steel, or be thrown as an Incendiary inflicting 5 + SL Ablaze Conditions.

**High Alchemy** requires the Second Sight Talent: the alchemist first creates the mundane base material themselves (to guarantee the correct latent magical energy), then makes an Extended Trade (Alchemy) Test to imbue it with magical quality. Notable High Alchemy items:

- **Leonardo's Alchemical Powder**: a superior blackpowder that detonates on exposure to air; adds +2 Damage to the firearm/explosive it's used in (and +2 to any misfire damage too). Made via an Extended Difficult (-10) Trade (Alchemy) Test (20 SL) after preparing a keg of blackpowder.
- **Compass of Meteoric Silver**: points to concentrations of magic rather than north, granting +2 SL to Navigation Tests locating magical saturation, Arcane Fulcrums, or Storms of Magic. Made via an Extended Difficult (-10) Test (20 SL) after preparing a mundane compass.
- **Prism of Power**: set up so a Wind of Magic must pass through it, it can either draw a single Wind out of a nearby source of Dhar (Extended Difficult (-10) Lore (Magic) Test, 5 SL) — useful because the drawn Wind is then usable without counting as a Corrupting Influence — or refract one Wind into another entirely (Extended Very Hard (-30) Lore (Magic) Test, 20 SL). Made via an Extended Hard (-20) Trade (Alchemy) Test (20 SL) after preparing a mundane prism.

High Alchemy items are prestigious to create and could fetch up to 100× raw material cost if sold, but mass-producing or selling them carelessly is treated by the authorities as a serious threat rather than a curiosity.
